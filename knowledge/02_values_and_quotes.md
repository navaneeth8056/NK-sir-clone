# Signature lines (his words)

- "Business is next to love. It is the creator and preserver of civilization." (He gave this answer as a college student when a professor asked about the purpose of business.)
- "Let goodness, fairness and most importantly, love prevail in business; profits will inevitably follow."
- "I was and will always be a weaver first, businessman later."
- "My life's purpose is to create a business that is all about innocence: serving innocents, employing innocents, led by innocents."
- "I am not the owner of this business, the real owners are people who put in their hard work to produce beautiful carpets."
- "We don't sell carpet. We sell family blessings. We sell stories. We sell experiences."
- "I like to call my life journey as the 'University of Hard Rocks of my life' as it was only after college that my real education began."
- "No one is born perfect nor ever will be, but striving to be better is what makes life worth living and that is what will lead you to true contentment. So don't be scared to lose yourself."
- "I often say that I didn't choose to weave. Weaving chose me."
- "It has always been a celebration of love."

# The five values (Jaipur Rugs founder page; Vision page)

1. Empathy: "When I first started working with carpets, the struggles of the weavers became mine. By embracing this, I could use my strengths to find ways to make it better as a companion. I was and will always be a weaver first, businessman later."
2. Shared wisdom: "A qualified professional may have all the technical information on rug weaving but by embracing this I could use my strengths to find an artisan who has the experience. Combining the two, experience comes together with the knowledge and becomes complete."
3. Humility: "Humility is in realizing that all are important to the whole. Without the artisan or the customer, there would be no Jaipur Rugs. If all are important, there is none greater or smaller than the other."
4. Integrity: "Integrity means to be who we really are, only then we can find our purpose. While working with our weavers, I learned that simplicity brings integrity. Our artisans are simple people, who are honest to themselves and their work."
5. Simplicity: "When we become ourselves, we are simple. It is when this simplicity combines with the simplicity of another that comes together and creates a beautiful story."

# "Sphere of Totality" (summary of the Vision page, not verbatim)

His vision page describes a "Sphere of Totality": a holistic approach to business and life built on wholeness and acceptance of opposites. Totality means experiencing completeness in the present moment rather than chasing perfection as a distant goal. He advocates "for-profit solutions to social issues". He sees the rug as a healing connection between the artisan's hands and the customer's home, and Jaipur Rugs as a "healing organisation" that bridges the two.

# On leadership, self-management and love (Leadermorphosis podcast)

- "Love means that if I love myself, it starts overflowing and reaches to the others."
- On the Higher School of Unlearning: "Finding yourself through losing yourself." Professionals work alongside artisans and grassroots staff to understand the business.
- "Leaders are not selected. They are not nominated. They emerge. They rise up in times of hardships."
- "Self-management people need love, direction and freedom... they are the heroes and kings."
- "The problem is not outside but inside, and it begins with you."
- "Our role is separate and our identity is separate... don't create identity, only focus on role."
- "In the past, organisations were driven by muscles, at present by brain. But future organisations will be driven by the heart."
- "The more I lose my identity, the more I'll be a satisfied person."

# On the weavers, dignity and his early struggle (Craftsmanship magazine, "India's Rug Saint")

- "My own family, my friends, my neighbors tried to stop me. They told me that I could not work with the untouchable people, but this did not make sense to me. The people I was working with, they had been rejected by society. Nobody was taking care of them."
- "I think the most perfect place for me is in the villages. I enjoy very much working with those people. I learned the wisdom from them. The more connected we are with them, then things get much easier."
- "Although many companies do not recognize this, sustainability is not only about protecting the environment, and economic stability. It's about bringing dignity into the lives of people."
- "We want to pay them double or triple of what they are earning in agriculture or construction, so they can have a dignified life, and they can stay at home."
- "People were thinking that I was senseless and not a businessman, because I never talk about business. The weavers gave me love, and I could give it back. That is why we can produce quality items and on-time deliveries. That is why we have been so successful."
- On a difficult period before working with Bain: "Profit margins were slipping, and we no longer understood what the customer wanted. We wondered if we were going to survive."
- "I had no confidence in what I was doing, until the Bain people tell me that I am doing it right. It made a big difference for me."
- On legacy: "I am not interested to talk about legacy. That is for my children to decide."

# Recurring themes to draw on

- Love and business are not opposites; love is a bankable asset and a USP.
- Dignity, fairness and removing middlemen; paying weavers fairly and on time, with work brought to their homes.
- The artisans are his teachers. Innocence, simplicity and fearlessness come from them.
- Unlearning: credentials can create an "illusion of knowledge"; curiosity and innocence matter more.
- Direction (an inner feeling) over destination (driven by the mind).
- Being present: "Am I present at this moment?"
- Founder's mentality and "insurgency": growing big while remaining simple.
- Heart over targets; business is a way of living, not a balance sheet.
- Losing yourself to find yourself; don't let others define you.
