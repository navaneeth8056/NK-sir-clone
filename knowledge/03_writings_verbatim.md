# Simplicity is the Key to Peace (8 Nov 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/founders-mentality/simplicity-is-the-key-to-peace/

I started my business with a vision to find my inner self. Today, "Jaipur rugs", what the world perceives as a complex business, was simply built on a transparent and uncomplicated piece of my will. I believe that my clear vision and simplicity of thoughts have made my search for solutions easier.

Vision gets converted to desires when the mind takes charge of thoughts. The desire, in turn, leads to disappointments and complications. Because somewhere we have misunderstood that life is the fulfillment of desires.

When a world is full of complexities and conflict, simplicity is the way I choose.

Simplicity is the key to peace. All the beauty of life lies in simplicity. A life lived in harmony inspires me to understand that peace of mind is achieved by extending simplicity in thought, word, and deed.

"Being outstanding does not always require moving against the flow; just being simple is enough." Being simple is difficult, though. It requires sincerity and commitment. Realizing and accepting the world as it is, makes our mind a place of positivity and peace.

For me, life is all about living in gentleness, love, compassion, and simplicity in action.

# Define Yourself or Get Defined (30 Jun 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/life-lessons/define-yourself-or-get-defined/

Who am I? A question we should be asking ourselves.

People often introduce and address me as a social entrepreneur, a businessman, CEO, founder, leader, and so on. The truth is that they know about me, but they don't know me.

Why do we connect our identity with our profession, financial status, and family background, when our life journey is more about our experiences and learnings? When something major happens in our lives, positive or negative, sometimes our perception, or the perception of those looking at us changes, and we have to redefine ourselves. Instead, we settle for what's easy and marketable.

When the outside world doesn't connect with our story, they paint an image so different from the life we lived. This creates a disconnect and we get lost in the crowd. I believe that labels and taglines given by society inhibit a person from achieving their true potential by not letting them make mistakes and earn new experiences.

I started my journey with two looms and nine artisans. I believe it's my Artisans uniqueness that made me what I am today. Sometimes, we need to trust the process and give in to earn the experience. Only then will we be able to reconsider our "I am" list.

The past four decades have been a unique journey filled with a lot of trials and tribulations but it has always been a celebration of love. So, don't let other people define you. Your story is a lot more magical than you give credit.

# Am I Present (12 Jun 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/life-lessons/am-i-present/

How do you act when things go wrong unexpectedly?

Well, a few days back, I came back home with a troubled heart and a frustrated mind. I was upset because of not being able to bring the results that I expected out of myself.

After introspecting upon my feelings on a deeper level, I realised that humans are prisoners of their own thoughts. The lens through which we perceive ourselves is often colored by external biases, ideologies, experiences, and established management practices.

This made me analyse myself. I was more involved in the doing than being, making me a slave to my habits.

So, I developed a new habit. I started asking myself whether I am present at this moment. This keeps me grounded, and makes me conscious of my being.

Sometimes learning comes from within. But, rarely do we pay attention. Only when we mute the noise will we be able to listen to our inner voice.

It will be challenging but don't be scared to lose yourself in the process. The person that emerges out of the chaos might just surprise you.

# Purpose is Timeless (19 May 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/healing/purpose-is-timeless/

Often during long walks around my hometown (Churu) in the evenings, I used to wonder about life and the purpose of living. Don't we sometimes unknowingly, become a contradiction of what we actually wish to be?

Well, the search for a purpose never ends. While some go on in search of their purpose, many others keep jumping from one thing to another. Being a businessman has given me the freedom to explore and experiment. However, before I decided to pursue a business I was in a dilemma.

For several days, I kept reading and meditating for the answers. But, only later did I realise that the answers were within myself. Once I listened to my heart things became clear and gradually it helped me to find the purpose of my life.

Over the years of creating Jaipur Rugs, I realised that business is a way of living and not a balance sheet that gives out profits. For this very reason, I have always followed my heart and not targets.

This reminds me of an incident a few years ago, when an ex-inmate of Jaipur Central jail was shifted to Open jail and on the very first day, he came to meet me. Vahid Khan came to request to set up a loom in the open jail since we had few looms set up in the central jail before. Within a few days, we installed a loom for him. Vahid was convicted in his twenties, an incident he so deeply regrets. Weaving gave him a second chance in life and he believes he has evolved as a person in the past few years.

Though I started my journey in 1978 on the road to sell, today I'm on the road to healing.

It has been a wild journey filled with a lot of trials and tribulations but it has always been a celebration of love.

# Acknowledge Deep Work and Selfless Passion in Employees (9 Apr 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/founders-mentality/acknowledge-deep-work-and-selfless-passion-in-employees/

Did you know, in our daily life there are almost five to ten people who make our day-to-day life easy and simple with their super skills? But, we are too disconnected and unconscious to even accept or appreciate them.

While I sit in my cabin and go through my daily schedule, I wonder if not for the selfless passion of my employees, my business would be in ruins otherwise.

Deep work makes every move you make purposeful. This reminds me of my 'Search for the Divine Soul' team, who through their perseverance brings candidates that are perfectly in sync with our ideas.

It is the employees who take the business forward but what do we as leaders do to make the journey fulfilling for them?

As leaders, we should be conscious of our actions and being, only then we can lead responsibly and empathetically. Whenever I meet my employees, I make it a habit to appreciate them and make them understand how important their role is in pushing the business forward.

Many a times, due to our ego and inferiority complex, we become blind to the powers we possess and the impact our actions have on others. Accepting our mistakes and shortcomings is what will take us forward.

If I have five people working under me for eight hours then I am not just responsible to give them a salary but also make sure that they are happy in their space and are able to infuse their purpose with that of the organization.

Money might give them financial stability but appreciating them fuels their passion and motivates them to be better.

I believe human connections are the biggest asset any leader should possess, the rest will follow eventually.

# Importance of Direction in Business (26 Feb 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/jaipur-rugs-journey/importance-of-direction-in-business/

After a year of uncertainty, I was really excited to meet my new set of divine heroes in person. After listening to their understanding and interacting for an hour I asked, 'what is the destination you seek in your role here?'

Within a few seconds, I received a couple of answers where many were saying they want to make several changes in the present system and how they will use their knowledge to transform the challenges into opportunities.

After which I asked them since you are sure about your destination, are you also aware of the direction you want to go?

There was an abrupt silence, while some began to think deeply, others looked blank waiting for me to say something.

With a smile I told them, direction is an inner feeling, a moment while the destination is driven by the mind.

Yes, it's that simple! My beginning in business too happened the same way.

I often say that I didn't choose to weave. Weaving chose me.

I didn't seek the destination when I decided to weave instead my weavers showed me the direction. I just followed them. Growing up with them made me realise that one of the major challenges they face is the exploitation of middlemen. Eliminating them from the system became my priority.

For this, I adopted a different model where the weavers were paid twice and on time while giving them a chance to work from home by providing them with all the raw materials they need.

This made them happier and more productive. People discover their best selves when they are treated with fairness and dignity, and especially when given a chance to earn a sustainable livelihood with the work of their hands.

At that time if I focussed on my destination then I don't think today Jaipur Rugs would even exist.

So my suggestion to you is to focus on the direction and eventually you will reach your destination.

Start with what you have, build a path with your understanding and it will direct you to the destination you want to reach.

# Rekindle Insurgency in Business (23 Feb 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/founders-mentality/rekindle-insurgency-in-business/

Creating a business enterprise is like having a child. The founder acts as a parent, nurtures the organization as one would do, imparts knowledge and values and then eventually, takes a less participatory role in the hopes that their efforts will result in success.

My business has always been close to my heart as I sacrificed quite a lot to see it flourish. In addition to turning down a potentially stable job, I was shunned by the community and derided by family members. This meant that I had little in terms of support during the early years.

What I lacked in support was more than amply made up in passion and enthusiasm, personally and from the weavers who joined my quest. So, it goes without saying that Jaipur Rugs is really like a child to me and passing on the same insurgency mission that I had and still have to another person is quite challenging.

While caught up in this dilemma a question that kept on coming back to me is 'Can something grow big and remain simple?'

As children when we grow up, we distance ourselves from our parents and family because we think their role in our life has ended. We grow up and learn a lot of things and during our journey, we realise something is missing. This missing leads to an emptiness in us which paves way for an imbalance in our life.

Similarly while establishing our business we go through this same phase. I have always believed that business should be infused with love. Therefore for me passing of the torch, the insurgent mission is about enabling my artisans to dream big by remaining simple. For when we grow big we need to make sure our roots are still intact on the ground otherwise it will lead to imbalance and ultimately break us down.

Rekindling insurgency is like going back to your parents and reestablishing the connection with them, watering the roots.

Today when I look back I feel closer to my purpose and it is my artisans who made me realise that something can grow big and remain simple.

Remember "insurgency is like the color of cloth. The lustre fades eventually but the colour remains the same." So make sure to take the necessary measures to preserve the lustre.

# Key Entrepreneurship Lessons
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/leadership/key-entrepreneurship-lessons/

There's a booming interest in entrepreneurship education lately, and its advocates claim that there's more science behind the subject these days. But I think that much of what modern entrepreneurship classes explain and teach, the best ways to avoid mistakes, is misleading.

Teaching budding entrepreneurs to avoid risks causes them more damage. They are lured by falling into never-ending planning and product manufacturing, without practical research or trialing.

Failures and mistakes are unavoidable and are the same as testing theories and learning in the scientific world. Just as we would never teach scientists to avoid experimenting, we shouldn't teach entrepreneurs to avoid making mistakes and risking failure.

Entrepreneurs refine their craft through experimentation and alliances in the real world. They learn best by rolling up their sleeves to take risks and building their companies while surrounding themselves with a reassuring mentor and peer community.

We can't teach entrepreneurship to anyone in the traditional manner. But we must create ways to make entrepreneurs help themselves to learn more efficiently. This implies discovering ways to provide them with a network of mentors and creative freedom to cultivate a business culture around them that articulates the following concepts: dream without barriers, open doors to creativity and innovation by listening to people, experiment more, trust, and be trusted, dare to make mistakes, treat others impartially and pay it forward.

While taking up the project Manchaha we were initially a little anxious since our artisans were new to designing. However, they proved us wrong and made us realise that their creative sense goes beyond one's imagination. They didn't need anyone to teach them what to design instead took inspiration from their surroundings. While they weaved passionately what they required was guidance and mentorship to enhance their capabilities.

This reminds me of a comment from International Designer Runa Ray: "The artisans are showing the corporates a way to move forward. The beauty of the designs is the colorful ways in which the artisans use their mind."

The risk behind giving artisans a chance to design as they wish indirectly gave birth to a creative movement. This articulates the concept that experimenting and taking risks paves way for innovations. Instead of avoiding mistakes entrepreneurs should learn from their mistakes and it gets better each time we fail.

Functioning this way means looking a little deeper than the traditional focus and discovering ways to nurture the communities that surround us. It's a change that can pay incredible dividends.

# Threads of My Life (23 Feb 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/jaipur-rugs-journey/threads-of-my-life/

It is often said that nature uses only the longest threads to weave her designs so that each small portion of her fabric divulges the organization of the entire textile.

My love affair with weaving began in 1978. I started my career selling shoes from a little shop that my father had started. I observed that there was very little room to grow.

Later I was offered a full-time job in a nationalized bank which I declined since I wanted to do something of my own where I could enjoy what I do. However, I was drawn to the art of weaving.

I borrowed Rs. 5000/- from my father to buy looms and an old cycle to travel to weaver's houses for day-to-day work. The beginnings were humble yet filled with challenges.

I felt this business best suited me because I could travel, and go to the villages and interact with weavers. The region was known for its preservation of heritage crafts, weaving being one of them.

When the first loom was set up, I enjoyed watching and admiring the dexterity of the 9 weavers, their fingers as they sent the shuttle flying back and forth, plying their magic was mesmerizing.

In this day of throwaway products, there is enough reason to have deep appreciation and gratitude for anything handcrafted.

Most of these crafts and arts are dying off. So when those who love handmade items get together, there is an intensity, energy, and passion that you don't find in many places.

A 14/14 rug can take three weavers working 6 days a week, ten hours a day, nearly a year to weave a hand-knotted rug. That is a piece of someone's life you have on the floor: a piece of their heart and soul. And that doesn't even take into consideration the shearing of the wool, dyeing of the wool, spinning of the wool before they even begin to weave the rug.

Hard work, dedication and effort have made the dream of Jaipur Rugs possible. And I must say, it has been a celebration of love from the beginning.

# Fearlessness stems from a sense of Belonging (23 Feb 2021)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/founders-mentality/fearlessness-stems-from-a-sense-of-belonging/

Fearlessness is driven by selflessness. It's my artisans who taught me how to be fearless.

Whenever I am fearful of something, I think about the talented artisans who are a tremendous inspiration to me and who galvanized me at a time when I was discouraged.

Like most entrepreneurs, I took a very big risk when I turned down a stable job in return for starting my own business. There was a lot of uncertainty. I was newly married. I didn't have the support of many family members. It would have been understandable if I gave up because of uncertainty.

The unknown can be scary but don't let fear stop you from following your dreams.

The artisans alleviate my fears. By observing them I realised that fear is driven by ego and inferiority complex. Instead, they were the personification of fearlessness.

These people do not have fear because they have little to lose. They are working from a pure place and with love as their motivation because their only goal is to provide for their families. These talented artisans create rather than produce. Unlike the rest of us, their motivations are selfless.

It was this incorruptible way of working that I witnessed that gave me the courage to move forward.

I understood then that if these artisans, who had so much talent and yet survived on so little, could work with dignity and grace, then what is to stop me?

It is quite normal to be apprehensive. In fact, if you weren't afraid of something, it would be strange. However, as leaders, we shouldn't let ego and inferiority complex take the best of us.

Being open, honest and fearless can be just the inspiration and cohesive cultural attribute that your organization can benefit from.

# This is the time to pause, to introspect and to reflect! (25 Jul 2020)
Source: https://www.nkchaudhary.com/this-is-the-time-to-pause-to-introspect-and-to-reflect/

People around the world are in a panic, and they need healing. This disastrous infiltration of Covid-19 reflects that for a long time now humanity has forgotten to take a pause, to reflect, and to acknowledge the fundamental reason for their co-existence. Perhaps now, is the time to do so.

For years, businesses and economies around the globe have been running through increasing consumption and producing wastage. This pattern has to be stopped now. This is the time to break our autopilot mode of life because if we continue like this then the end to the world would be definite.

The old and ancient wisdom of the world has always advocated that when you get everything, you come back to the basics, to the basic human needs.

I believe though a challenging time, it's also an opportunity that nature has brought to all of us to slow down and begin our journey inside. Businesses need to re-evaluate how can they bring love and care to their customer, because only the one who brings it, will survive.

Viruses like this may come and go, but now is the best time to introspect about what brought us here. Know that this is not the battle against a virus; it's the battle against our past conduct.

Stay safe, stay indoors and remember that, "this too, shall pass".
